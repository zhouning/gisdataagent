# ADR-026: Lead-Time and Information-Vintage Contract

**Date**: 2026-07-27  
**Status**: Accepted as an evaluation contract; current rank-one candidate rejected by the registered core-horizon gate

## Context

ADR-025 rejected contemporaneous NWM covariance propagation at a one-hour target. That result was insufficient to decide
whether the graph correction had no dynamic value or merely acted at the wrong travel delay. The same public Center Hill
development window was already outcome-exposed, so it could support a preregistered diagnostic but not a new prospective
claim. The graph gain could not be tuned again on this window.

A second ambiguity concerned future information. NWM retrospective `q_lateral` and realized CWMS releases exist for the
whole window, but they are not forecasts that were available at an historical issue time. Treating them as operational
forcing would create look-ahead leakage even if the outlet target itself were never passed to the model.

## Decision

Freeze lead times `1/3/6/12/24h` before executing a multi-horizon rollout. One hour is diagnostic; `3/6/12/24h` are the
non-compensatory core horizons. Every issue time has all five horizons and uses a common complete-case mask per horizon.

At each issue time, each scenario receives only observations available under the registered one-hour publication-lag
assumption. The scenario analysis is cloned into one continuous 24-hour rollout. No observation after issue time may be
assimilated, and the branch rollout may not mutate the hourly cycling state. The unchanged graph, local multi-gauge, and
outlet-only scenarios are compared with:

- causal latency-matched persistence: latest observation available at issue time, held to the target horizon;
- zero-latency archive persistence: archive observation ending at issue time, held to the horizon, explicitly diagnostic
  because it is unavailable under the lag assumption.

Two information tracks are separate by construction:

1. `retrospective_oracle_forcing`: realized future release and retrospective future `q_lateral`; executable only as an
   archive-replay diagnostic;
2. `operational_forecast`: not executable until historical issue-time NWM forecast forcing, release-plan vintages, and
   observation publication vintages exist. Its metrics must remain null.

The core development gate requires graph RMSE to be strictly below local multi-gauge, outlet-only, and causal persistence
at all four core horizons, plus conservation for every executed step. One-hour results cannot change that gate.

## Consequences

The 24-hour sign change is evidence that geographic propagation has a delay-shaped effect, but one positive horizon in
an exposed single-system window is not validation. The rank-one contemporaneous basis remains rejected and must not be
tuned to emphasize 24h.

The next candidate must be a dynamic graph state estimator with memory, not a horizon-specific multiplier. It should use
only issue-time and earlier observation history, preserve the frozen DAG support, and maintain a single coherent state
whose forecasts at all horizons are generated by the same transition. Any reconstructed analysis mass must enter the
ledger once; it cannot be duplicated across downstream reaches or horizons. Lag support must come from topology,
hydraulic travel-time priors, or outcome-free modeled-state dynamics before target scoring.

The operational data gap is now explicit rather than silently filled with realized future values. Public data can still
be acquired by the project, but until historical forecast vintages are found, only oracle-forcing archive replay is
scientifically available.
